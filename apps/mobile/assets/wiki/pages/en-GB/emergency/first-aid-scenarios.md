# Festival first-aid scenarios

> **CALL 999 OR 112 NOW** for severe breathing difficulty, inability to speak or cough, sudden throat or tongue swelling, uncontrolled heavy bleeding, collapse, a seizure, inability to stay awake, or serious head-injury signs. Do not wait for the festival team before calling.

Send someone to the nearest festival medical point or ask a steward, security or welfare team to bring medics. Give your exact location and stay with the person. Welfare can provide support and help find services, but serious symptoms need medical help. If someone is unresponsive or not breathing normally, follow [Get emergency help](wiki:emergency.get-help).

## Severe bleeding

1. Press firmly and continuously on the wound with a sterile dressing or clean non-fluffy cloth.
2. If an object is embedded, do not remove it; press around it instead.
3. Call 999 or 112 and send for festival medics. Keep the person warm and keep checking their response and breathing.

Do not improvise a tourniquet unless the emergency call handler tells you to and guides you.

## Choking in an adult

- If they can breathe, speak or cough, encourage them to keep coughing.
- If they cannot breathe, cough or make a sound, give up to 5 sharp back blows, then up to 5 abdominal thrusts.
- If the blockage remains, call 999 or 112 and alternate 5 back blows with 5 abdominal thrusts until help arrives.
- If they become unresponsive, follow [Get emergency help](wiki:emergency.get-help) and start CPR when directed.

Do not sweep inside the mouth with your fingers; remove an object only if it is clearly visible.

## Anaphylaxis

Sudden swelling of the lips, mouth, throat or tongue; difficulty breathing or swallowing; a hoarse voice; confusion; faintness or collapse can be anaphylaxis.

1. Help the person use **their prescribed adrenaline auto-injector immediately**, following the instructions on it.
2. Call 999 or 112, say "anaphylaxis", and send for festival medics.
3. Keep them lying down. If breathing is difficult, they may raise their shoulders or sit up slowly. Do not let them stand or walk.
4. If symptoms have not improved after 5 minutes, help them use their second prescribed auto-injector if available.

## Asthma attack

Sit the person upright, reassure them, and help them use **their prescribed reliever inhaler** and spacer, following their personal asthma action plan or inhaler instructions.

Call 999 or 112 if they are getting worse, are exhausted, cannot speak normally, look blue, grey or very pale, have no inhaler, or do not improve after the maximum dose in their plan. Send for festival medics early and keep checking their breathing.

## Serious head injury or possible spinal trauma

Call 999 or 112 after a head injury if the person was knocked out and has not woken, cannot stay awake, has a seizure, has weakness or numbness, has trouble with vision, balance, walking, speaking or understanding, has clear fluid from an ear or nose, is bleeding from an ear, or has a dent or embedded object in the head.

Keep them still and support their head without twisting it. **Do not move them or ask them to move after a possible spinal injury**, unless they are in immediate danger or movement is needed to keep their airway open. Airway and breathing take priority; follow the call handler's instructions.

## Burns and scalds

1. Stop the burning process and move away from danger.
2. Cool a heat burn under cool or lukewarm running water for at least 20 minutes.
3. Remove nearby clothing and jewellery unless stuck to the skin. Keep the rest of the person warm.
4. After cooling, cover loosely with cling film laid over the burn or a clean non-fluffy dressing.

Do not use ice, creams, oils or butter, burst blisters, or pull off stuck clothing. Send for festival medics. Call 999 or 112 for a very large or deep burn, a burn to the face or genitals, breathing difficulty, significant smoke inhalation, or a chemical or electrical burn.
