# Stoves, fire, carbon monoxide and burns

## Prevent fire and carbon monoxide

- Never use a barbecue, camping stove, heater or other fuel-burning appliance inside a tent, awning, vehicle or other enclosed space, even after the flames appear out.
- Follow the event's rules. Use permitted equipment only outdoors, on a stable clear surface, away from tents, dry vegetation, fuel and walkways.
- Never leave a flame unattended. Keep fuel closed and away from ignition sources.
- Keep tent entrances, guy lines and paths visible to reduce trips and blocked escapes.

Carbon monoxide has no colour or smell. Headache, dizziness, nausea, weakness, confusion, chest or muscle pain, and shortness of breath can be signs of poisoning. Symptoms may improve outside and worsen on returning.

## Suspected carbon monoxide

1. Stop using the appliance only if this can be done without danger.
2. Get everyone into fresh air immediately.
3. Do not re-enter the tent or enclosed space.
4. Tell festival medical and security staff what was burning and where.
5. Call 999 or 112 for breathing difficulty, chest pain, confusion, collapse, seizure or unconsciousness. Seek prompt medical advice for any suspected exposure.

Do not enter a smoky or unsafe space to rescue equipment or another person. Alert staff and emergency services.

## Burn or scald first aid

1. Stop the burning process and move away from danger.
2. Remove nearby clothing and jewellery unless stuck to the skin.
3. Cool the burn under cool or lukewarm running water for 20 minutes as soon as possible.
4. Keep the rest of the person warm without covering the burn.
5. After cooling, cover loosely with cling film laid over the burn, or a clean non-fluffy dressing. Do not wrap cling film around a limb.

Do not use ice, iced water, butter, toothpaste, creams or greasy substances. Do not burst blisters or pull away stuck clothing.

## Get urgent help

Call 999 or 112 for severe breathing problems, collapse, significant smoke or fume inhalation, or a major burn with signs of shock.

Go to festival medical staff and A&E for chemical or electrical burns; burns larger than the person's hand; deep burns; white or charred skin; or burns to the face, neck, hands, feet, joints or genitals. Children and people with significant medical conditions should also be medically assessed.

If the person is unresponsive and not breathing normally, start CPR and follow [Get emergency help](wiki:emergency.get-help).
