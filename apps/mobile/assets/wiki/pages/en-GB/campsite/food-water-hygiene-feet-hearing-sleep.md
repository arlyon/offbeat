# Food, water, hygiene, feet, hearing and sleep

## Food, drinking water and hands

- Use event-designated drinking water. Do not assume water from a hose, stream or unmarked tap is safe.
- Follow food labels and vendor instructions. Keep food that needs chilling cold, separate raw food from ready-to-eat food, and discard food with damaged packaging or uncertain storage.
- Wash hands with soap and water after using the toilet and before preparing or eating food. Use hand sanitiser when washing facilities are unavailable, but wash visibly dirty hands as soon as possible.
- Do not prepare food for others while you have vomiting or diarrhoea.

For vomiting or diarrhoea, rest and take small sips of fluid. A pharmacist can advise on oral rehydration solution. Do not share towels, utensils or drinks.

Seek NHS 111 or festival medical help for bloody diarrhoea, inability to keep fluids down, severe or constant abdominal pain, signs of dehydration, symptoms lasting longer than expected, or worsening illness. Call 999 or 112 for vomiting blood, severe breathing difficulty, collapse or unresponsiveness.

## Cuts, blisters and feet

- Wear well-fitting footwear and change wet socks. Check sore areas before they become open wounds.
- Rinse small cuts with clean running water, clean the surrounding skin, pat dry and cover with a sterile dressing.
- Cover a blister with a soft dressing or blister plaster. Do not deliberately burst it or peel off the skin.
- Get medical advice if a wound remains dirty, was caused by a bite or contaminated object, will not stop bleeding, has something embedded, loses feeling or movement, or becomes redder, hotter, more swollen, more painful or produces pus.

People with diabetes, poor circulation or reduced sensation should seek clinical advice early for foot wounds.

## Bites and stings

Move away from the insect, remove a visible sting by scraping sideways, wash the area, and apply a cold pack wrapped in cloth. Do not scratch.

Call 999 or 112 for swelling of the lips, mouth, tongue or throat; breathing or swallowing difficulty; sudden confusion or severe dizziness; fainting; or blue, grey or pale skin. Use an adrenaline auto-injector if the person has one and follow its instructions.

## Hearing

Use well-fitted earplugs near loud music, take quiet breaks and move farther from speakers. Ringing or muffled hearing means reduce exposure. Sudden hearing loss in one or both ears needs urgent medical assessment; persistent ringing or hearing change needs NHS advice. See [Protect your hearing at festivals](wiki:campsite.festival-hearing-protection) for earplug choice, fit, cleaning and rating limits.

## Sleep and fatigue

Plan a quiet, warm and dry sleep period. Earplugs should still allow emergency announcements or a buddy wake-up plan. Caffeine, alcohol and other drugs do not replace sleep and may worsen sleep quality or judgement.

Do not drive, cook on a stove, swim or climb when struggling to stay awake. If someone is unusually difficult to wake, confused or breathing abnormally, treat it as an emergency rather than ordinary tiredness.
