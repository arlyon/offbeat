# Nutrition, supplements and recovery

## What supports recovery

- Eat regular, ordinary meals or snacks with carbohydrate, protein, fruit or vegetables, and some salt according to your usual needs.
- Rest in a cool, quiet place and get real sleep. Do not drive if sleep-deprived or impaired.
- Drink regularly according to thirst, heat, activity and fluid losses. Avoid both dehydration and forced excessive drinking.
- After vomiting or diarrhoea, a pharmacist can recommend oral rehydration solution. Electrolyte products are not needed for everyone and do not make unsafe drug use safe.

## Caffeine and energy products

Caffeine can worsen anxiety, tremor, palpitations and sleep. Check labels because coffee, tablets, powders, pre-workout and energy drinks can add up. Do not exceed label directions or mix products to fight severe tiredness. Chest pain, collapse, severe agitation, confusion, seizure or an abnormal heartbeat needs urgent medical help, not another stimulant.

## Vitamins, minerals and detox products

Supplements do not replace meals, sleep or medical care. More is not necessarily better: high doses of some vitamins and minerals can cause harm. Follow the label and ask a pharmacist about pregnancy, health conditions and medicine interactions.

Magnesium, antioxidant megadoses, detox drinks and multi-product recovery stacks are not established treatments for drug toxicity and should not be relied on for recovery. Product purity and combined doses may also be uncertain.

## 5-HTP and serotonergic risk

Do not use 5-HTP as claimed protection against MDMA neurotoxicity. Human evidence does not establish that it prevents such harm, and there is no proven safe post-MDMA timing or dose for that purpose.

5-HTP is a serotonin precursor. Combining serotonergic substances may increase the risk of dangerous serotonin toxicity. This includes MDMA and can include antidepressants, tramadol, some cough medicines, St John's wort and other serotonergic products. Do not add 5-HTP or St John's wort to a drug recovery stack, and do not stop prescribed medicine to take a supplement. Ask a pharmacist or prescriber before any supplement if you take medicines.

Agitation, confusion, heavy sweating, overheating, diarrhoea, muscle twitching or rigidity, seizure, collapse or rapidly worsening illness after drugs, medicines or supplements needs urgent medical assessment. Call 999 or 112 for severe symptoms.

## When self-care is not enough

Persistent low mood, anxiety, sleeplessness, pain, vomiting, confusion or physical weakness needs professional advice. Be honest about all drugs, medicines and supplements. Supplements must never delay festival medical care, NHS 111 or emergency help.
