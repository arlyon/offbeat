# Welfare, safeguarding, spiking and buddies

## Consent and immediate safety

Consent must be freely chosen and can be withdrawn at any time. Someone who is asleep, unconscious or too impaired to choose cannot consent. Previous consent, a relationship, clothing, alcohol or drugs do not imply consent.

If someone may be in danger, move to a staffed, well-lit place if safe. Contact a steward, welfare team, security or festival medical point. Call 999 or 112 for immediate danger, serious injury, breathing problems, collapse, seizure, unresponsiveness or a person at imminent risk of harming themselves or someone else.

## Suspected spiking

Sudden unexpected intoxication, confusion, poor coordination, memory gaps, vomiting, hallucinations or unusual drowsiness may have many causes and need medical assessment.

1. Stay with the person and believe what they report.
2. Take them to festival medical or welfare staff promptly.
3. Do not let them leave with an unknown person, wander off, drive or take more alcohol, drugs or medicine.
4. Tell medics what they may have consumed. Keep a drink container only if safe, but never delay care to collect evidence.
5. Testing windows vary, so ask staff or police promptly if the person wants testing or to report.

Spiking is never the victim's fault. Do not interrogate them or pressure them to report to police.

## After sexual assault

The person's immediate safety and choices come first. Offer a quiet place, ask before touching, and let them choose who stays. Sexual assault referral centres can provide medical care, emergency contraception, testing, emotional support and forensic examination without requiring an immediate police report.

If the person may want forensic evidence collected, avoiding washing or changing clothes can preserve options, but care must never be withheld and they should not be blamed if they have washed, changed, eaten or drunk. Bag clothing separately in paper if specialist staff advise it. Contact festival welfare or the NHS sexual assault service for the local route.

## Panic, psychosis or suicidal crisis

Reduce noise and crowds, speak calmly, listen, and do not argue about unusual beliefs or restrain the person unless trained staff must prevent immediate harm. Seek festival welfare or medical help. Stay with someone who says they may act on suicidal thoughts and remove immediate hazards only when safe.

For urgent mental health help in England, use NHS 111 and select the mental health option. Call 999 or 112 when there is immediate danger.

## Buddy plan and missing people

Before separating, agree a staffed meeting point and times, share campsite landmarks, and decide who will alert staff. Keep an emergency contact written down in case a phone is lost or flat.

Report a missing child, vulnerable adult, person in medical danger, or disappearance that is out of character to festival staff immediately. Do not organise an unsafe search. Without phone signal, go directly to a steward, security post, welfare tent or medical point and give the person's description, last known location and relevant risks.
