# Protect your hearing at festivals

## Act now for sudden hearing loss

- **A sudden drop in hearing in one or both ears:** leave the noise and seek an urgent GP appointment or NHS 111 now. Do not wait until the festival ends or sleep on it. NICE says unexplained loss developing over three days or less within the past 30 days should be assessed by ENT or an emergency department within 24 hours.
- **Tinnitus with sudden hearing loss, facial weakness or vertigo, or tinnitus after a head injury:** go to A&E or call 999. See [Get emergency help](wiki:emergency.get-help).
- **Regular or constant tinnitus, or tinnitus that is worsening or troubling you:** arrange a GP appointment. Ask for an urgent GP appointment if the sound beats with your pulse.

Ringing, buzzing or muffled hearing after loud music means the exposure was too high. It may improve, but do not assume it is harmless or expose your ears to more loud sound while waiting.

*Sources: NHS hearing-loss and tinnitus guidance; NICE NG98.*

## Reduce exposure at every loud stage

- Put hearing-protection earplugs in **before** entering the loud area and keep them in while it remains loud.
- Move well away from speakers and amplifiers. There is no universal safe distance: stage layouts, reflections and levels vary. If you must raise your voice to be heard by someone an arm's length away, keep moving to a quieter area.
- Take at least **10 minutes in a genuinely quiet place every hour**. The back of another loud crowd is not a quiet break. Breaks reduce exposure; they do not erase earlier exposure or guarantee recovery.
- Exposure adds up across sets, stages and days. Venue limits and occupational noise limits manage population or workplace risk; they are not guaranteed safe attendee durations.

*Sources: WHO safe-listening guidance and venue standard; HSE hearing-protection guidance.*

## Foam or filtered earplugs?

Both can protect when they are designed and rated for hearing protection, fit correctly and stay in.

- **Disposable foam:** inexpensive and capable of useful attenuation when fully inserted and sealed. Fit is technique-sensitive, and music may sound less clear.
- **Filtered or musician earplugs:** designed to preserve music and speech clarity more evenly; they may be universal-fit, reusable or custom-made. “Musician”, “filtered” or “high fidelity” does not make a plug inherently safer. Tested attenuation and a reliable seal still matter.

Do not use swimming plugs, ordinary noise-masking products or cotton wool as substitutes for rated hearing protection. Bring a spare pair rather than relying on venue supplies.

*Sources: RNID hearing-protection guidance; WHO safe-listening guidance.*

## Fit foam plugs: roll, pull, insert, hold

1. Move somewhere quieter if possible. With clean, dry hands, roll the foam into a narrow shape.
2. Reach over with the opposite hand and pull the outer ear up and back.
3. Insert the plug into the ear canal to a comfortable depth—not just across the opening.
4. Hold it while it expands and seals. Fit both ears.
5. If it loosens, move somewhere quiet and refit or replace it.

For pre-moulded, filtered or custom plugs, use that product's fitting method. Do not force any plug through pain or marked discomfort.

*Sources: WHO safe-listening guidance; HSE hearing-protection guidance.*

## Hygiene, reuse and ratings

- Use disposable plugs once. Reuse only plugs explicitly sold as reusable.
- Clean reusable plugs only as their instructions specify; filters and materials differ. Inspect them for dirt, damage, hardening or a poor seal, and replace them when needed.
- Never share plugs. Fit with clean hands and store reusable plugs clean and dry in their case.
- Stop inserting plugs and seek medical advice for ear pain, bleeding, discharge or a new hearing change.

SNR and frequency-band ratings come from standardised laboratory tests. Real protection is often lower because ear canals, fit, loosening, wear and brief removal differ. **Do not subtract the package SNR from a phone's dB reading** to claim a personal at-ear level or safe duration: the rating, weighting and measurement position do not support that calculation. A phone app also does not measure the seal under your plug.

*Sources: RNID hearing-protection guidance; HSE hearing-protection and real-world-factor guidance.*
