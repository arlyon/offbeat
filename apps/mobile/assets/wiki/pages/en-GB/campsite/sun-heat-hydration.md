# Sun, heat and hydration

## Reduce heat and sun exposure

- Use shade, take breaks from dancing, and wear loose, light clothing plus a hat.
- Use at least SPF 30 sunscreen with at least 4-star UVA protection. Reapply every 2 hours and after swimming or heavy sweating.
- Drink regularly, guided by thirst, activity, heat and losses. There is no single safe festival fluid quota.
- Never pressure someone to drink large amounts quickly.

## Heat exhaustion: act now

Headache, dizziness, nausea, heavy sweating, cramps, thirst, weakness, pale or clammy skin, and fast breathing or heartbeat can be heat exhaustion.

1. Move the person to a cool or shaded place.
2. Remove unnecessary outer clothing.
3. Cool their skin with cool water, fanning, or cold packs wrapped in cloth at the neck and armpits.
4. If fully alert and able to swallow, offer cool water or an oral rehydration or sports drink in manageable sips.
5. Stay with them. They should start improving within 30 minutes.

## Call 999 or 112

Call now if the person is confused, poorly coordinated, has a seizure, loses consciousness, is not breathing normally, has hot skin that is not sweating, or is still unwell after 30 minutes of cooling and fluids. These can be signs of heatstroke.

Do not give anything by mouth to someone who is drowsy, confused, vomiting repeatedly or unconscious. Use [Get emergency help](wiki:emergency.get-help).

## Dehydration, vomiting and diarrhoea

Dark urine, urinating less, dizziness, tiredness, dry mouth and intense thirst can indicate dehydration. Take small sips if nausea makes drinking difficult. A pharmacist can recommend oral rehydration solution when vomiting or diarrhoea has caused fluid and salt loss.

Seek NHS 111 or festival medical help if the person cannot keep fluids down, remains dizzy when standing, is unusually drowsy or confused, has very dark or little urine, or is getting worse.

## MDMA and overhydration

MDMA can disrupt temperature and water balance. Both dehydration and drinking too much water can be dangerous. Excess water can dilute blood sodium, and there is no fixed amount that is safe for everyone.

Do not force water, follow drinking challenges, or keep drinking despite feeling unwell. Severe headache, repeated vomiting, confusion, marked drowsiness or a seizure after MDMA or heavy fluid intake needs urgent medical help. Tell medics what was taken and roughly how much was drunk. Do not wait for certainty.
