# Cold, rain and severe weather

Festival instructions and steward directions take priority. Conditions, shelter plans and evacuation routes vary by event.

## Keep warm and dry

- Carry a waterproof outer layer and warm layers. Replace wet clothes and socks when possible.
- Keep a dry sleep layer and sleeping bag protected inside waterproof storage.
- Eat regular meals and avoid relying on alcohol for warmth. Alcohol increases heat loss and can hide deterioration.
- Check friends who are wet, exhausted, intoxicated, isolated or unable to care for themselves.

## Suspected hypothermia

Warning signs include shivering, pale cold skin, slurred speech, slow breathing, tiredness or confusion. Severe hypothermia may stop shivering.

1. Call 999 or 112 and alert festival medical staff.
2. Move the person to shelter if this can be done safely.
3. Remove wet clothing, wrap them in dry layers or blankets, and cover the head.
4. Keep them awake. If fully awake and able to swallow, a warm non-alcoholic drink and sugary food may be offered.
5. Stay with them and monitor breathing.

Do not use a hot bath, hot-water bottle, heat lamp, skin rubbing or limb massage. Do not give alcohol. If unconscious but breathing, use the recovery position; if not breathing normally, start CPR and follow [Get emergency help](wiki:emergency.get-help).

## Thunder and lightning

- Follow venue announcements immediately. Do not delay an evacuation to pack.
- If staff direct you to substantial shelter, use it. A tent or gazebo is not reliable lightning protection.
- Move away from isolated trees, exposed high ground, fences, stages, lighting towers, water and metal structures.
- Do not shelter beneath a tree. Avoid touching wet ropes, poles or metal barriers.
- Wait for the event to give the all-clear before returning.

## High wind, damaged tents and flooding

Leave a tent or gazebo if poles are failing, fabric is tearing, water is entering rapidly, or nearby trees or temporary structures look unstable. Move to the designated safe area and tell staff about hazards.

Do not camp or remain in flowing or rising water. Do not wade into floodwater to recover property. Keep away from power equipment, cables and fallen structures. If trapped, injured, or unable to reach the event's safe area, call 999 or 112 or send someone to a steward.

Weather warnings describe regional risk, but only the event can confirm local closures, evacuation routes and safe shelters.
