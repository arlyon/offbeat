# Other stimulants: speed, cathinones and methamphetamine

> **Get emergency help now** for severe chest pain, collapse, seizure, dangerous overheating, stroke-like signs, abnormal breathing, loss of consciousness, a very fast or irregular heartbeat, or severe agitation, paranoia or confusion that creates immediate danger. Follow [Get emergency help](wiki:emergency.get-help) and do not wait to identify the stimulant.

This class guide covers several different stimulants. Their names, appearance and effects cannot confirm identity, strength or purity. A powder, crystal, paste, capsule or tablet may contain another stimulant, a mixture or an unexpected drug. The sections below are not interchangeable.

## Speed and amphetamine

In the UK, "speed" usually refers to illicit amphetamine, often sold as powder or paste. A wet-looking product, colour, smell or seller's claim does not show how much amphetamine it contains. Products sold as base or sulphate can vary substantially and may contain caffeine or other substances.

Amphetamine can raise heart rate, blood pressure and body temperature and suppress sleep and appetite. Serious poisoning can involve abnormal heart rhythm, chest pain, stroke, seizure, overheating, severe agitation, paranoia or psychosis. Snorting can damage nasal tissue; injecting adds vein damage, infection and blood-borne virus risk.

## Mephedrone, 4-MMC and other cathinones

Mephedrone is also called 4-MMC, but 3-MMC, 2-MMC and other synthetic cathinones are distinct substances. A market name or test result suggesting one cathinone does not establish that exact cathinone, and an amount associated with one must not be transferred to another. European monitoring has found increasing cathinone variety and mismatches between the cathinone expected and identified; those European signals are not UK festival-prevalence estimates.

Cathinones can cause stimulant harms including fast heart rate, raised blood pressure, overheating, agitation, anxiety, paranoia, seizures and compulsive repeated use. Some also have serotonergic activity, so unknown identity makes medicine and drug interactions harder to predict. Snorting can injure the nose, and injecting adds infection and blood-borne virus risk.

## Methamphetamine, crystal meth and tina

Methamphetamine is distinct from the amphetamine usually sold as speed. It can produce prolonged stimulation, making repeated use, missed sleep, overheating, dehydration and delayed mental-health deterioration especially concerning. Severe agitation, paranoia, hallucinations or psychosis can emerge or worsen after extended wakefulness and may continue when the person expects the drug to have worn off.

Methamphetamine is used in some sexualised-drug-use settings, but this is not characteristic of any sexuality or community. Long sessions, impaired judgement and loss of sleep can increase injury, infection and consent risks. Injecting or sharing needles, pipes or other equipment can transmit infection or cause tissue injury. Use sterile equipment from a needle and syringe service, never share it, use barriers appropriate to the activity, and check that consent remains clear and freely given. Significant intoxication or unconsciousness means a person cannot consent.

## Shared risks and combinations

Combining any of these stimulants with cocaine, MDMA, another stimulant, large amounts of caffeine, some decongestants or prescribed stimulants can add cardiovascular, agitation, seizure and overheating risk. Sedatives do not cancel stimulant toxicity and can create an additional poisoning risk or hide deterioration.

Some cathinones and methamphetamine can affect serotonin. Combining unknown stimulants with MDMA, MAOIs, tramadol, certain antidepressants or other serotonergic drugs creates uncertain and potentially serious risks. Do not stop prescribed medication abruptly in order to use a stimulant; ask a pharmacist or prescriber about interactions. See [Dangerous drug combinations](wiki:drug-testing.dangerous-combinations).

Heat, crowds, prolonged exertion, poor sleep and uncertain product strength make harm less predictable. Confusion, heavy sweating, high temperature, rigid muscles, tremor, diarrhoea or seizure may be serotonin toxicity, heat illness or another emergency. Do not try to diagnose the cause before seeking help.

## Reduce immediate harm

The lowest-risk option is not to use. Drug checking may reveal a red flag but cannot prove identity, purity, strength, dose or safety. Do not use taste, numbness, appearance or effects to identify a stimulant. See [Professional and at-home drug checking](wiki:drug-testing.professional-and-at-home).

Avoid mixing substances and do not use another drug to counter agitation, chest symptoms or sleeplessness. Move away from heat and exertion if symptoms begin, stay with the person and involve festival medical or welfare staff early. Do not force fluids or use a fixed drinking target: both dehydration and excessive water intake can be dangerous. Do not give drinks to someone who is confused, very drowsy or unable to swallow normally.

Do not share straws, pipes or injecting equipment. Keep unknown powders sealed and separate from medicines, food and drink. If symptoms are unexpected, treat the product as unknown and see [Unknown substances and counterfeit products](wiki:drug-testing.unknown-and-counterfeit).

## After use and longer-term support

Anxiety, low mood, poor sleep, exhaustion and paranoia can follow stimulant use. Persistent chest symptoms, severe headache, confusion, hallucinations, worsening paranoia, thoughts of self-harm or inability to sleep with deteriorating mental health need clinical assessment. Immediate danger requires [Get emergency help](wiki:emergency.get-help).

Repeated use can lead to craving and dependence. A GP, sexual-health service or local drug service can provide confidential support and blood-borne virus testing without requiring abrupt unsupported change.
