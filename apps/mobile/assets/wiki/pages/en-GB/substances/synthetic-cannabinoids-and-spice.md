# Synthetic cannabinoids, Spice and SCRAs

> **CALL 999 OR 112 NOW** if someone has collapsed, cannot be woken, is not breathing normally, has a seizure, has severe chest pain, is dangerously hot, has severe agitation or psychosis creating immediate danger, or is rapidly getting worse. Follow [Get emergency help](wiki:emergency.get-help). Do not wait for the product to be identified or assume the reaction is ordinary cannabis intoxication.

Synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonists, or SCRAs, are changing groups of laboratory-made chemicals that act at cannabinoid receptors. Spice, K2, Mamba and similar names are market labels, not one drug. Products sold under the same name, or even in the same packet, can contain different chemicals or uneven concentrations.

> **SCRAs are not simply stronger cannabis.** Their effects can be more severe and unpredictable than cannabis, and there is no class-wide composition, strength, dose or duration that can be treated as reliable or safe.

## Severe and unpredictable poisoning

Effects can include confusion, panic, paranoia, hallucinations, psychosis, extreme drowsiness, severe agitation or aggression. Physical signs can include repeated vomiting, heavy sweating, tremor, seizures, a very fast or irregular heartbeat, high blood pressure, chest pain, collapse and unconsciousness. Serious kidney injury and deaths have also been reported.

A person may change quickly between agitation and reduced consciousness. Stay with them, keep the area as calm as possible and remove nearby hazards only when safe. Do not restrain them unless needed to prevent immediate injury, argue about unusual beliefs, make them walk, put them in a shower, make them vomit, or give cannabis or another drug to counter the effects.

If they are unconscious but breathing normally, put them in the recovery position and keep checking their breathing. If they are not breathing normally, start CPR and follow the emergency call handler. Persistent vomiting, dehydration, continuing confusion or concerning mental-health symptoms also need medical assessment even without an immediate 999 sign.

## Unknown THC, CBD and HHC products

A vape, refill, edible or herb sold as THC, CBD, HHC or cannabis may contain a synthetic or semi-synthetic cannabinoid, several substances, or contaminants instead. Branding, flavour, smell, liquid colour, packaging and effects cannot establish what is inside. Ingredients and concentration may vary between units sold together.

Home tests cannot exclude every current or newly appearing cannabinoid, identify every mixture or prove that an unregulated product is safe. A test result must not override symptoms. See [Unknown substances and counterfeit products](wiki:drug-testing.unknown-and-counterfeit), [Professional and at-home drug checking](wiki:drug-testing.professional-and-at-home) and [Cannabis](wiki:substances.cannabis).

## Dependence and withdrawal

Regular use can lead to tolerance and dependence. Stopping after frequent use can cause cravings, irritability, anxiety or other mood changes, poor sleep, reduced appetite, sweating, shaking, diarrhoea and feeling very unwell. The product's unknown and changing contents make a generic withdrawal timetable or class-wide reduction plan unsafe.

A GP or local drug service can help plan support and assess physical or mental-health symptoms. Do not use an unknown street product to manage withdrawal. A seizure, collapse, severe confusion, psychosis creating danger, suicidal crisis or immediate risk of harm requires [Get emergency help](wiki:emergency.get-help).

## Combinations and immediate safety

Alcohol, opioids, benzodiazepines, GHB/GBL and other sedatives can add unconsciousness, vomiting, aspiration and breathing risks. Stimulants may add overheating, agitation, chest pain and heart-rhythm risks. No other drug makes an SCRA product predictable or protects against poisoning. See [Dangerous drug combinations](wiki:drug-testing.dangerous-combinations).

Tell festival medical staff what the product was sold as, the form it took, when exposure may have happened, and what other drugs, alcohol or medicines may be involved. Keep packaging or the device for responders only when safe, but never delay care to find it.
