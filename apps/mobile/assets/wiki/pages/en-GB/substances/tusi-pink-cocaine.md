# Tusi, Tuci and pink cocaine: unknown mixtures

> **Treat the product as unknown.** Tusi, Tuci, Tusibi, Tucibi and "pink cocaine" are market labels, not a chemical identity. Colour, branding, price, taste, smell, effects or a seller's claim cannot establish what is present.

If someone is unconscious, cannot be woken, is not breathing normally, has a seizure, collapses, has severe chest pain, is dangerously hot, has stroke-like signs or is rapidly worsening, follow [Get emergency help](wiki:emergency.get-help) now. Do not wait to test the powder or work out which ingredient caused the symptoms.

## The name does not define the contents

The label does not establish the presence or absence of cocaine, 2C-B, ketamine, MDMA, cathinones or any other drug. It does not identify a recipe, drug class, strength or purity. Composition can differ between sellers, batches and portions of the same mixture. Pink colouring is not evidence of any ingredient.

There is therefore no reliable Tusi dose, effects profile, duration, redosing interval or interaction chart. A report about one analysed sample cannot be transferred to another. Feeling stimulated, sedated, dissociated or psychedelic cannot identify the mixture and does not show that other drug classes are absent.

Reliable UK prevalence is uncertain. This warning is included because an unpredictable multi-class mixture changes emergency and testing decisions, not because Tusi is established as common at UK festivals.

## Act on observable danger

An unknown mixture can produce overlapping or changing signs. Use the signs, not the product name:

- **Slow, abnormal or stopped breathing; blue or grey lips or skin; gurgling; limpness; or inability to wake:** follow [Get emergency help](wiki:emergency.get-help) immediately. Possible depressant or opioid poisoning cannot be excluded. If naloxone is available, use it according to its instructions or your training while help is called, but do not wait for a response or assume it treats the whole mixture.
- **Severe chest pain, collapse, seizure, a very fast or irregular heartbeat, dangerous heat, stroke-like signs, or severe escalating agitation:** follow [Get emergency help](wiki:emergency.get-help) immediately. Do not try to counter the symptoms with a sedative or stimulant.
- **Confusion, heavy sweating, high temperature, rigid muscles, tremor, diarrhoea or seizure:** this may be serotonin toxicity, heat illness or another emergency. Seek immediate help rather than diagnosing the mixture.

See [Dangerous drug combinations](wiki:drug-testing.dangerous-combinations) for the emergency patterns created by depressant, stimulant and serotonergic substances. The product may create a combination even when the person did not intend to mix drugs.

## Drug checking is only a red-flag tool

A reagent colour, strip, appearance or effects cannot identify all components, measure each amount or prove safety. A negative opioid strip cannot rule out every opioid, another depressant or contamination elsewhere in the powder. A result suggesting one ingredient cannot rule out others.

Professional analysis can identify more components, but its result applies to the submitted portion and may not represent another portion or batch. Festival testing may be back-of-house rather than a personal service, and WEDINOS is not an immediate emergency service. See [Professional and at-home drug checking](wiki:drug-testing.professional-and-at-home).

An unexpected, incomplete or unavailable result is a reason not to use the material, not a reason to guess. Symptoms always override a test result.

## Practical harm prevention

The lowest-risk option is not to use an unknown mixture. Do not taste, sniff or take a small amount to identify it. Keep the powder sealed, labelled and separate from medicines, food, drinks and known substances. Avoid spreading dust or touching the face, and wash hands after accidental contact.

Do not mix it with alcohol, medicines or other drugs, and do not take another substance to balance an unexpected effect. If exposure may have happened, have a sober person stay, avoid roads, water, heights, heat and crowds, and involve festival medical or welfare staff early. Do not give food or drink to someone who is confused, very drowsy or unable to swallow normally.

Tell responders every substance and medicine that may be involved. Keep packaging, a photograph or a sample for them only when safe and without delaying care. See [Unknown substances and counterfeit products](wiki:drug-testing.unknown-and-counterfeit).
