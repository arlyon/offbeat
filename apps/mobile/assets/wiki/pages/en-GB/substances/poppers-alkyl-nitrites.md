# Poppers and alkyl nitrites

> **Call 999 or 112 now** for collapse, inability to wake, abnormal or difficult breathing, blue or grey lips or skin, seizure, severe chest pain, sudden severe visual loss or rapid worsening. Follow [Get emergency help](wiki:emergency.get-help). Blue or grey colour can mean the blood is not carrying oxygen normally even when fresh air does not help.

Poppers are volatile alkyl-nitrite liquids intended to release an inhaled vapour. Products may contain different nitrites, and a brand name, bottle or smell does not establish the chemical or strength. Use occurs across different communities and settings; this guide does not associate it with one identity or sexuality.

## Never swallow poppers

Swallowing poppers can cause severe poisoning, including methaemoglobinaemia, in which blood cannot carry oxygen normally. Tell festival medical staff immediately after any suspected swallowing, even if the person initially appears well. Do not make them vomit or give food, drink or a home remedy. Severe or worsening signs require [Get emergency help](wiki:emergency.get-help).

Headache, dizziness, confusion, unusual drowsiness, breathlessness, collapse, or blue or grey lips, skin or nail beds can be signs of serious poisoning. Symptoms matter more than whether the product was swallowed, inhaled or spilled.

## Dangerous interaction with erection medicines

Do not combine poppers with sildenafil, tadalafil or vardenafil, commonly sold as Viagra, Cialis and Levitra, or with another PDE5 inhibitor. Together they can cause a profound, potentially life-threatening fall in blood pressure, fainting and collapse. The interaction can still matter when a medicine was taken earlier; do not use a timing rule to declare the combination safe.

Alcohol and other substances or medicines that lower blood pressure may add dizziness or fainting risk. Do not stop prescribed cardiovascular or other medicine abruptly. Ask a pharmacist or prescriber about interactions.

## Fainting, falls and reduced oxygen

Alkyl nitrites widen blood vessels and can cause headache, flushing, dizziness and a sudden fall in blood pressure. Sit or lie somewhere away from roads, water, heights, fire and crowds if dizziness starts. Do not stand someone up or give another drug to counter faintness.

Collapse, persistent confusion, breathing difficulty, blue or grey colour, seizure or inability to wake is not an ordinary rush. Get emergency help and tell responders that an alkyl nitrite may be involved.

## Liquid contact, fire and chemical injury

The liquid can injure skin and eyes, and products are flammable. Keep the bottle closed and away from flames, sparks, hot surfaces, lit cigarettes and ignition sources. Prevent splashes and direct liquid contact with the mouth, nose, skin and eyes. Keep bottles away from drinks so they cannot be swallowed by mistake.

For skin or eye exposure, remove contaminated clothing if this can be done safely and rinse the affected area with plenty of cool or lukewarm running water. Do not apply creams or other chemicals. Involve festival medical staff or seek urgent clinical advice, especially for eye exposure, ongoing pain, blistering or a large affected area. Do not delay emergency help for severe injury or breathing problems.

## Visual symptoms

Alkyl nitrites have been linked to retinal injury, sometimes called poppers maculopathy. Blurred or distorted central vision, a central blind spot, difficulty focusing or marked light sensitivity after use needs prompt eye assessment. Stop exposure and do not wait for repeated use or severe sight loss before seeking care. Sudden severe visual loss or visual symptoms with collapse, severe headache, weakness or confusion requires [Get emergency help](wiki:emergency.get-help).

## Reduce immediate harm

The lowest-risk option is not to use. Never swallow the liquid, never decant it into a drink container, and do not combine it with PDE5 inhibitors. Avoid mixing substances, keep away from ignition sources and do not use near roads, water, heights or other fall hazards. Stay with anyone who is unwell and tell medical staff the product name, what exposure may have occurred and what other medicines or drugs may be involved.

There is no imported dose or duration record for poppers. Product identity and concentration vary, and no amount, breath count or timer can establish safety or justify delaying help.
