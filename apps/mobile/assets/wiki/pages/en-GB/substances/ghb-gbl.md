# GHB and GBL

GHB and GBL are central nervous system depressants commonly sold as liquids. GBL is converted to GHB in the body, but the substances and products are not interchangeable. Concentration can vary greatly between bottles and batches, and appearance or taste cannot establish contents or strength.

The margin between desired effects and severe poisoning can be small and unpredictable. Effects can be delayed or vary with food, health, tolerance, other drugs and the actual concentration. No volume, calculator or timer can make use safe.

## Main risks

GHB/GBL can cause confusion, loss of coordination, vomiting, seizures, sudden unconsciousness and suppressed breathing. A person who appears deeply asleep may be poisoned. Vomiting while unconscious can block the airway.

Memory loss and severe impairment increase vulnerability to assault. A person who is significantly intoxicated cannot consent to sex or other decisions.

## Dangerous combinations

Alcohol, opioids, benzodiazepines, ketamine and other sedatives can combine with GHB/GBL to cause unconsciousness, abnormal breathing and death. Stimulants do not protect the airway or make depressant poisoning safe and may mask warning signs.

Do not mix GHB or GBL with alcohol or other depressants. See [Dangerous drug combinations](wiki:drug-testing.dangerous-combinations).

## If someone is very drowsy

Stay with them and keep them away from water, traffic, heights and other hazards. Do not give food, drink, coffee or another drug. Do not put them in a shower or bath, make them vomit or assume they can sleep it off.

If they cannot be woken or their breathing is abnormal, follow [Get emergency help](wiki:emergency.get-help) immediately. Airway and breathing matter more than knowing the amount or waiting for a predicted effect time.

## Other emergency signs

Seizure, collapse, blue or grey skin or lips, repeated vomiting with reduced consciousness, severe agitation, chest pain or rapid worsening also requires [Get emergency help](wiki:emergency.get-help) now.

## Dependence and withdrawal

Frequent use can cause dependence. GHB/GBL withdrawal can become severe quickly and may include agitation, confusion, hallucinations, tremor, sweating, fast heart rate, seizures or delirium. Do not advise a dependent person simply to stop abruptly without medical support.

Seek urgent medical assessment for suspected dependence or withdrawal. Severe confusion, hallucinations, seizure, collapse or immediate danger requires [Get emergency help](wiki:emergency.get-help).
