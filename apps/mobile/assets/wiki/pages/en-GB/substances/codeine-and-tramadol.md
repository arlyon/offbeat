# Codeine and tramadol

> **CALL 999 OR 112 NOW** if someone is unconscious, cannot be woken, has slow, shallow, irregular or stopped breathing, has blue or grey lips or skin, makes snoring or gurgling sounds, is limp, has a seizure, collapses or is rapidly getting worse. Follow [Get emergency help](wiki:emergency.get-help). Do not wait for certainty or let them "sleep it off".

Codeine and tramadol are opioid painkillers. Either can suppress breathing and consciousness. A prescription, tolerance or familiar-looking packet does not prevent overdose, and tablets bought outside a regulated pharmacy may contain something else.

## Act now: breathing and naloxone

1. Call 999 or 112 and say the person is unresponsive or not breathing normally. Tell the call handler that an opioid may be involved and send someone for festival medical staff and an AED.
2. Open the airway and check breathing. If they are not breathing normally, start CPR and follow the call handler. If they are unconscious but breathing normally, use the recovery position and keep checking.
3. If naloxone is available, give it promptly according to its supplied instructions or your training. Continue breathing support or CPR; further naloxone may be needed, so follow the product instructions and call handler rather than improvising.
4. Stay until the ambulance arrives. Even if they wake, naloxone can wear off before the opioid. Do not leave them alone or let them take another tablet.

Naloxone does not treat paracetamol or NSAID poisoning, a tramadol-related seizure, serotonin toxicity, alcohol or other sedatives. It never replaces breathing support or an ambulance. Tell responders every product, medicine, alcohol or drug that may be involved and bring the packets when safe.

## Prescribed medicine and non-medical use

Take prescribed codeine or tramadol only as directed on the label and by the prescriber. Do not take extra, change the schedule, share it or use another person's supply. Ask a pharmacist or prescriber about alcohol, over-the-counter products, antidepressants and other medicines rather than guessing at an interaction or stopping treatment.

Codeine and tramadol are not interchangeable. Tramadol has seizure and serotonergic risks that cannot be inferred from codeine, while codeine's conversion to morphine creates its own uncertainty. Neither record on this page describes all opioids or establishes what is in a non-prescribed tablet.

## Codeine response is difficult to predict

The body converts codeine into morphine using an enzyme called CYP2D6. Genetic differences mean some people convert very little and get little pain relief, while others can produce morphine more rapidly and face greater toxicity, including dangerous sedation and breathing suppression. Past experience, body size, appearance or another person's response cannot reliably predict an individual's effect.

Do not take more because codeine seems not to work. More codeine can still cause harm, and a combination product may deliver a dangerous amount of its other ingredients before opioid effects are obvious.

## Combination products: every ingredient can poison

Codeine is sold in products combined with paracetamol, ibuprofen or aspirin. The full product name and every active ingredient matter. Opioid signs can be absent while another ingredient is causing serious harm.

- **Paracetamol combinations, including co-codamol:** taking more than directed can cause severe liver damage. A person may initially feel well. Seek urgent NHS advice immediately after taking more than directed; do not wait for symptoms.
- **Ibuprofen and other NSAID combinations:** taking more than directed can cause stomach or intestinal bleeding, ulceration and kidney injury as well as opioid poisoning. Aspirin-containing products carry their own additional poisoning risk. Black stools, blood in vomit, severe stomach pain or very little urine need urgent medical assessment.

Never exceed the packet or prescription directions, and do not add paracetamol, ibuprofen, aspirin or another painkiller without checking all active ingredients and asking a pharmacist or prescriber. If there is abnormal breathing, unconsciousness, seizure, collapse or rapid worsening, call 999 or 112. Bring all packets and remaining tablets when safe, but do not delay care to find them.

## Tramadol: seizures and serotonin toxicity

Tramadol can cause seizures and can contribute to serotonin syndrome. Risk may increase with MDMA and other serotonergic drugs, MAOIs, some antidepressants and other interacting medicines. Medicine interactions are complex; a short list cannot declare a combination safe.

Confusion, severe or worsening agitation, heavy sweating, high temperature, muscle rigidity, tremor, diarrhoea, a very fast heartbeat or a seizure may be serotonin toxicity or another emergency. Follow [Get emergency help](wiki:emergency.get-help) rather than trying to diagnose or counteract it. Do not stop an antidepressant or other prescribed medicine abruptly to take tramadol; ask a pharmacist or prescriber.

Alcohol, benzodiazepines or Z-drugs, pregabalin, gabapentin, GHB/GBL and other sedatives can combine with either opioid to suppress breathing and consciousness. Stimulants do not protect the airway. See [Dangerous drug combinations](wiki:drug-testing.dangerous-combinations).

## Counterfeit medicines and unknown tablets

> **A blister pack, pharmacy-style box, logo, score line or tablet imprint does not prove identity or strength.** A product sold as codeine, tramadol or another painkiller may contain a different opioid, including a potent synthetic opioid such as a nitazene, or a mixture.

Testing, appearance and seller claims cannot rule out every opioid or measure strength. If opioid exposure is possible, respond to abnormal breathing with 999 or 112, airway support and available naloxone regardless of what the packet says. See [Unknown substances and counterfeit products](wiki:drug-testing.unknown-and-counterfeit) and [Opioids and potent synthetic opioids](wiki:substances.opioids-and-potent-synthetic-opioids).

## Dependence, withdrawal and reduced tolerance

Codeine and tramadol can cause tolerance, physical dependence and addiction, including at prescribed doses. Abruptly stopping a prescribed opioid can cause withdrawal, with symptoms such as agitation, anxiety, sweating, sleep disturbance, aches, abdominal cramps, diarrhoea, nausea and vomiting.

> **Do not stop prescribed codeine or tramadol abruptly.** Ask the prescriber, a pharmacist or a drug service for an individual, supported reduction plan. Do not use counterfeit tablets or another opioid to manage withdrawal.

Tolerance can fall after stopping or reducing use. Returning to an amount previously used can then cause overdose. Severe dehydration, confusion, seizure, collapse, abnormal breathing or immediate danger needs urgent assessment; emergency signs require [Get emergency help](wiki:emergency.get-help).
