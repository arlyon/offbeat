# Benzodiazepines and street benzos

> **CALL 999 OR 112 NOW** if someone is unconscious, cannot be woken, is not breathing normally, has blue or grey lips or skin, makes snoring or gurgling sounds, has a seizure, collapses or is rapidly getting worse. Follow [Get emergency help](wiki:emergency.get-help). Do not let them "sleep it off" or wait to identify a tablet.

Benzodiazepines are sedative medicines that include diazepam, sold as Valium, and alprazolam, sold as Xanax in some countries. "Street benzos" may contain bromazolam, another benzodiazepine, a different sedative, a potent synthetic opioid or a mixture. Zopiclone and zolpidem are Z-drugs rather than benzodiazepines, but they share important sedation, dependence, withdrawal and combination risks.

## Unconsciousness, breathing and blackouts

Benzodiazepines and related sedatives can cause extreme drowsiness, confusion, poor coordination, slurred speech, memory loss and blackouts. Effects can be prolonged or return after someone appeared more alert. During a blackout, a person may move or speak but later remember little or nothing; this does not mean they were safe or able to consent.

Stay with a heavily impaired person and keep them away from traffic, water, heights, fire, cold and crowd pressure. Do not give food, drink, coffee or another drug, make them vomit, put them in a shower, or make them walk.

If they are unconscious but breathing normally, put them in the recovery position and keep checking. If they are not breathing normally, start CPR and follow the emergency call handler.

## Street tablets and counterfeit medicines

> **A logo, score line, colour, blister pack or tablet imprint never proves identity or strength.** A tablet sold as Xanax, Valium or another medicine may not contain the named drug, and tablets that look identical may differ.

WEDINOS has detected bromazolam and nitazenes in selected samples submitted as diazepam. These concern-driven submissions show that substitution can happen; they do not estimate how common it is in the wider UK supply. An old alert, seller claim, photograph or negative test cannot rule out a current contaminant.

See [Unknown substances, counterfeit products and opioid contamination](wiki:drug-testing.unknown-and-counterfeit) and [Professional and at-home drug checking](wiki:drug-testing.professional-and-at-home). Testing is a red-flag tool, not proof that a tablet is genuine or safe.

## Dangerous combinations

Alcohol, opioids, GHB/GBL, gabapentin, pregabalin and other sedatives can combine with benzodiazepines or Z-drugs to suppress consciousness and breathing. Ketamine adds severe impairment, vomiting, injury and aspiration risk. A stimulant may mask sleepiness but does not protect breathing.

Inability to wake someone or abnormal breathing is an emergency even if the amount seemed small or the tablet was prescribed. See [Dangerous drug combinations](wiki:drug-testing.dangerous-combinations).

If opioid exposure is possible and naloxone is available, give it according to its supplied instructions or your training while someone calls 999 or 112. Naloxone only reverses opioid effects; it does not reverse benzodiazepines, Z-drugs or other sedatives and does not replace breathing support or an ambulance.

## Dependence and withdrawal

Benzodiazepines and Z-drugs can cause tolerance and physical dependence, including when prescribed. Abruptly stopping or sharply reducing them can trigger withdrawal. Symptoms can include severe anxiety, agitation, confusion, sweating, tremor, vomiting, hallucinations and seizures.

> **Do not stop prescribed benzodiazepines or Z-drugs abruptly.** Ask the prescriber, a pharmacist or a drug service for an individual, supported reduction plan. Do not use street tablets to manage withdrawal because their contents and strength are unknown.

A seizure, severe confusion, hallucinations that create danger, collapse or immediate risk of harm requires [Get emergency help](wiki:emergency.get-help). Tell responders about prescribed medicines, street products, alcohol and other drugs, and when they may have been taken. Keep packaging for staff when safe, but never delay care to find it.
