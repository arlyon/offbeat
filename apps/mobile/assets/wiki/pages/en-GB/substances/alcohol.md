# Alcohol

Alcohol is a depressant. Drinks vary in strength and serving size, and intoxication can continue to worsen after a person stops drinking. Tolerance can change how intoxicated someone appears but does not prevent poisoning.

## Main risks

Alcohol impairs coordination, judgement, memory and reaction time. It increases risk around traffic, water, heights, fire and cold, and can contribute to falls, head injury, violence and missing signs of another illness or drug effect.

Vomiting while very drowsy or unconscious can block the airway. A person who is severely impaired cannot consent to sex or other decisions. Memory gaps do not establish consent.

## Dangerous combinations

Alcohol with opioids, GHB/GBL, benzodiazepines, ketamine or other sedatives can suppress breathing and consciousness. Alcohol with cocaine adds cardiovascular toxicity and does not cancel impairment. Stimulants may make someone feel more awake while alcohol poisoning continues.

See [Dangerous drug combinations](wiki:drug-testing.dangerous-combinations).

## Alcohol poisoning

Confusion, repeated vomiting, inability to stand, pale or blue-tinged skin, slow or irregular breathing, seizure, collapse, unconsciousness or inability to wake someone may indicate alcohol poisoning or another emergency.

Follow [Get emergency help](wiki:emergency.get-help) immediately for abnormal breathing, seizure, blue or very pale skin, collapse, unconsciousness or inability to wake the person. Do not wait for all signs to appear or assume they can sleep it off.

Coffee, energy drinks, cold showers and walking do not reverse poisoning. Do not make the person vomit or give food or drink to someone who is very drowsy or unconscious. Never put an impaired person in a bath or shower.

## Injury and vulnerability

If an intoxicated person has fallen or may have hit their head, watch for worsening confusion, repeated vomiting, seizure, weakness, severe headache or loss of consciousness. These require urgent medical assessment, and severe signs require [Get emergency help](wiki:emergency.get-help).

Stay with an impaired person, keep them away from hazards and involve welfare or medical staff early. Do not leave them with someone they do not trust.

## Dependence and withdrawal

Regular heavy drinking can cause dependence. Abruptly stopping after dependence can cause dangerous withdrawal, including hallucinations, seizures and delirium. Do not reduce this to advice to stop suddenly. Seek a GP or alcohol service for an assessed, supported plan. Severe confusion, hallucinations, seizure or collapse requires [Get emergency help](wiki:emergency.get-help).
