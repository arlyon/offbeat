# Cannabis

Cannabis products include flower, resin, oils, vapes and foods. THC and other cannabinoid content varies. A product sold as cannabis may contain synthetic cannabinoids, other drugs or contaminants. Appearance, smell, packaging and effects do not establish identity or strength.

## Main risks

Cannabis can impair coordination, reaction time, memory and judgement. It can cause anxiety, panic, paranoia, confusion, nausea, vomiting and a faster heart rate. Driving, cycling, swimming, cooking, heights and roads become unsafe while impaired.

Risk of severe anxiety or psychotic symptoms may be higher with high-THC products, frequent use, younger age, sleep deprivation, or a personal or family history of psychosis or bipolar disorder. Cannabis can interact with prescribed medicines. A pharmacist or prescriber can advise without requiring abrupt changes to treatment.

Smoking irritates the lungs. Vapes and oils from unregulated sources add uncertainty about ingredients and concentration.

## Edibles

Edible effects may begin later and last longer than inhaled effects. Uneven mixing and unclear labelling make portions unreliable. Do not use a lack of immediate effect as proof that a product is inactive or safe to take again. Keep edibles away from children and anyone who could mistake them for ordinary food.

## Synthetic cannabinoids are different

Products sold as cannabis, THC/CBD/HHC-style vapes or edibles may instead contain synthetic cannabinoids. Names such as Spice or K2 cover changing groups of chemicals rather than one predictable drug. These substances are not simply stronger cannabis and can cause more severe, unpredictable poisoning, including collapse, seizures, chest pain, severe agitation, psychosis and unconsciousness.

Packaging, flavour, liquid colour and a familiar-looking vape do not establish what is inside. Home tests cannot exclude every synthetic cannabinoid or prove that an unregulated vape is cannabis. A severe reaction should not be dismissed as ordinary cannabis intoxication. See [Synthetic cannabinoids, Spice and SCRAs](wiki:substances.synthetic-cannabinoids-and-spice) for class-specific poisoning and withdrawal guidance.

## Supporting mild distress

If the person is anxious but alert, breathing normally and not in immediate danger, move to a quiet place away from crowds, roads, heights and water. Have one calm sober person stay, reduce stimulation and reassure them simply. Do not argue about unusual beliefs or give another drug to counter the effects.

## Emergency signs

Severe chest pain, collapse, seizure, loss of consciousness, abnormal breathing, dangerous overheating, severe psychosis or agitation that creates immediate danger, or rapid worsening requires [Get emergency help](wiki:emergency.get-help) immediately.

Do not wait to see whether symptoms fit cannabis or try to identify the product from effects. Repeated vomiting, dehydration, persistent confusion or concerning mental-health symptoms also need medical assessment.

## Combinations and support

Alcohol and other drugs can increase impairment, vomiting, panic and injury risk. Psychedelics or stimulants may intensify distress. See [Dangerous drug combinations](wiki:drug-testing.dangerous-combinations).

Frequent use can lead to dependence. A GP or local drug service can support changes and assess sleep, anxiety, vomiting or mental-health problems.
