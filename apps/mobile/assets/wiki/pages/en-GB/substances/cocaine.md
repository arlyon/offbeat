# Cocaine

Cocaine is a stimulant sold in powder and smokable forms, including crack. Names, appearance and effects do not confirm identity. Strength varies and products may contain local anaesthetics, other stimulants or unexpected drugs.

## Main risks

Cocaine can raise heart rate, blood pressure and body temperature. Serious harms include abnormal heart rhythm, heart attack, stroke, seizure, severe agitation and overheating. These can affect young people and people without a known heart condition.

Snorting can damage nasal tissue. Smoking can harm the lungs. Injecting adds infection, vein damage and blood-borne virus risk. Frequent use can lead to anxiety, paranoia, low mood, craving and dependence.

## Combinations and setting

Alcohol does not cancel cocaine impairment. The body can form cocaethylene when both are present, adding cardiovascular toxicity. Combining cocaine with MDMA, amphetamines or other stimulants adds strain, agitation and overheating risk. Sedatives may mask some effects without removing toxicity.

Heat, crowded spaces, prolonged dancing, dehydration and unknown product strength make risk less predictable. See [Dangerous drug combinations](wiki:drug-testing.dangerous-combinations).

## Reduce immediate risk

The lowest-risk option is not to use. Do not use appearance, taste, numbness or a reagent colour to identify cocaine. Drug checking may reveal a red flag but cannot prove identity, purity, dose or safety.

Avoid mixing substances and avoid exertion if symptoms develop. Do not share straws, pipes or injecting equipment. Do not try to counter chest pain, agitation or sleepiness with another drug.

## Emergency signs

Severe chest pain, collapse, seizure, dangerous overheating, severe or worsening agitation, a very fast or irregular heartbeat, sudden weakness on one side, facial droop, speech difficulty, a severe sudden headache or loss of consciousness require [Get emergency help](wiki:emergency.get-help) immediately.

Do not wait for the effects to pass or try to confirm what was taken. Keep any packaging or sample for responders when safe.

## After use

Anxiety, low mood and disturbed sleep can follow stimulant use. Persistent chest symptoms, severe headache, weakness, confusion or worsening mental health need clinical assessment. A GP or local drug service can help with craving or dependence without requiring abrupt unsupported change.
