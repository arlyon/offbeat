# Gentle hip routine

For generally healthy adults, not treatment for pain or injury. Use a clear, level space. Stop immediately if a movement hurts or you feel unwell. Do not balance when dizzy, intoxicated, overheated or exhausted.

## Before walking or dancing

### Supported knee lifts

- **Set up:** Stand tall beside a secure fixed support. Keep the supporting knee slightly bent.
- **Move:** Lift one knee towards the opposite hand, lower with control, then alternate. Keep the back straight.
- **Amount:** Up to 30 alternating lifts in 30 seconds, as used in the NHS warm-up.
- **Easier:** Use a solid, non-wheeled chair and do the seated hip march below.

### Seated hip march

- **Set up:** Sit upright near the front of a solid, stable chair without wheels. Feet are flat and knees bent. Hold the chair sides.
- **Move:** Lift one bent knee only as far as comfortable, lower slowly, then change sides.
- **Amount:** 5 lifts per leg.
- **Easier:** Lift the foot only slightly or omit this movement.

## After activity, if comfortable

Only use ground positions on a dry, clean, level surface.

### Glute stretch

- **Set up:** Lie on your back with knees bent. Cross one ankle over the opposite thigh.
- **Move:** Hold behind the uncrossed thigh and gently draw it towards the chest.
- **Amount:** Hold 15 to 20 seconds, then change sides.
- **Easier:** Draw the thigh less close. Stop if the position strains the knee or hip.

### Inner-thigh stretch

- **Set up:** Sit upright with knees bent and soles of the feet together.
- **Move:** Hold the feet and let the knees lower only to a comfortable position. Do not push them down.
- **Amount:** Hold 15 to 20 seconds.
- **Easier:** Let the knees remain higher or omit the stretch.

## Do not use this routine

Do not use it for a new injury, unexplained pain, a hot or swollen joint, numbness, weakness, recent surgery, or when a clinician has limited movement or weight-bearing. Pregnancy and health conditions may need individual modifications.

Get festival medical or NHS 111 help for severe or worsening pain, inability to bear weight, major swelling, deformity, fever with a hot joint, or symptoms that do not settle. Call 999 or 112 after a serious injury with deformity, cold or blue skin, or loss of feeling.

Stretching may help comfort or flexibility, but it is not proven treatment and does not guarantee prevention of injury or next-day soreness.
