# Gentle ankle and calf routine

For generally healthy adults after long walking or standing, not treatment for an acute injury. Move away from mud, slopes, tent lines, glass and traffic. Keep footwear on unless the surface is known to be safe.

## Seated ankle point and flex

- **Set up:** Sit upright on a solid, stable chair without wheels. Hold the chair sides.
- **Move:** Straighten and raise one leg. Point the toes away, then draw them back towards you with control.
- **Amount:** 2 sets of 5 per foot, as used in the NHS sitting routine.
- **Easier:** Make a smaller pain-free movement or omit it.

This movement is for mobility and does not prevent or treat a blood clot.

## Supported calf stretch

- **Set up:** Face a secure wall or stable fixed support. Step one leg forward and bend that knee. Keep the rear leg straight and both feet pointing forward.
- **Move:** Lean slightly forward while trying to keep the rear heel down.
- **Amount:** Hold 15 to 20 seconds, then change sides.
- **Easier:** Shorten the step and reduce the lean. Omit the stretch if the ground or support is unstable.

Stretch only to a comfortable position. Do not bounce, force the heel down or use another unsteady person, tent pole, loose barrier or folding chair for support.

## Acute ankle injury

Do not stretch through a new sprain or strain. For the first 2 to 3 days, NHS advice is protection, rest from the activity, ice wrapped in cloth for up to 20 minutes every 2 to 3 hours, compression and elevation. Avoid heat, alcohol and massage during this early period. Resume movement only when pain does not stop it.

Get festival medical or NHS 111 help for very bad or worsening pain, major swelling or bruising, inability to walk more than a few steps, marked stiffness, or failure to improve.

Go to A&E or call 999 or 112 after injury if the ankle is at an odd angle, bone is visible, there is a bad wound, severe pain, numbness, or toes are blue, white or cold.

## One-sided calf warning

Throbbing pain and swelling in one calf or thigh, skin colour change, warmth or swollen veins may be a DVT rather than muscle tightness. Do not massage or stretch it. Seek urgent NHS 111 help. Call 999 or 112 if these signs occur with chest pain or breathlessness.

Stop any routine immediately if it causes pain or you feel unwell. It does not guarantee prevention of injury or next-day soreness.
