# Gentle knee-support routine

For generally healthy adults, not an injured knee. Use a clear, level space and a secure fixed support. Stop immediately if any movement hurts or you feel unwell. Never force a deep bend.

## Before walking or dancing

### Shallow supported knee bends

- **Set up:** Stand with feet shoulder-width apart and hold a secure support.
- **Move:** Bend both knees and lower no more than about 10 cm, keeping the back straight, then rise smoothly.
- **Amount:** Up to 10 repetitions, as used in the NHS warm-up.
- **Easier:** Make the bend smaller or omit it. Do not continue if the knee catches, gives way or becomes painful.

### Heel digs

- **Set up:** Stand tall with a slight bend in the supporting leg. Use support if needed.
- **Move:** Place one heel forward with toes pointing up, return, then alternate.
- **Amount:** Up to 60 alternating digs in 60 seconds.
- **Easier:** Slow down, reduce the number, or omit the movement.

## After activity, if comfortable

Only use ground positions on a dry, clean, level surface.

### Hamstring stretch

- **Set up:** Lie on your back with one knee bent and foot down. Raise the other leg and hold below its knee.
- **Move:** Keep the raised leg straight and gently draw it towards you.
- **Amount:** Hold 15 to 20 seconds, then change legs.
- **Easier:** Keep the raised knee slightly bent or draw it less close.

### Front-thigh stretch

- **Set up:** Lie on one side with knees together.
- **Move:** Hold the top foot and gently draw the heel towards the buttock, without twisting the knee.
- **Amount:** Hold 15 to 20 seconds, then change sides.
- **Easier:** Draw the heel less close. Omit it if reaching the foot is difficult or the knee is uncomfortable.

## Stop and seek help

Do not use this routine after an acute injury or with marked swelling, redness, heat, deformity, locking, giving way, numbness, weakness, or inability to bear weight.

Get festival medical or NHS 111 help if the knee is badly swollen, cannot move or bear weight, locks or gives way, has changed shape, or is hot and red with fever or feeling unwell.

One-sided calf or thigh pain with swelling, skin colour change or swollen veins may be a DVT, not tight muscle. Do not massage or stretch it. Seek urgent NHS 111 help. Call 999 or 112 if these signs occur with chest pain or breathlessness.

This routine does not repair injury or guarantee prevention of injury or next-day soreness.
